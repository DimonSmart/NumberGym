# IDD-0006.spec-shared-trainer-app-integration

## Intent

The product family supports multiple focused language-trainer applications that
share one consistent learning experience while each application owns its
identity and subject matter.

## Behavior

- Each trainer application supplies its own name, branding, app information,
  content catalog, and content-specific presentation where needed.
- Each content catalog supplies exercise families, cards, language material, and
  the interaction modes appropriate to that content.
- The shared trainer experience provides session lifecycle, scheduling, progress,
  settings, statistics, and common training screens.
- A training session publishes a coherent lifecycle state: an active exercise is
  present only while the session is active, and a completed session has no
  active exercise, disables interaction, and exposes its session statistics.
- The session phase is the sole source of truth for its lifecycle. Starting and
  stopping are temporary phases; after a public lifecycle operation finishes,
  the session is in a stable idle, paused, completed, or disposed state.
- A stop request lasts only while its serialized stop operation is pending. A
  stop while idle is a no-op and neither it nor a failed cleanup prevents a
  later start. Cleanup failures are reported after the session has normalized
  its visible state.
- The immediate cancellation signal is best effort and never throws to its
  caller. Its failures are retained for the owning exercise generation and are
  reported only as part of completed cleanup, after other cleanup work has had
  a chance to run.
- Runtime ownership operations independently serialize attachment, detachment,
  disposal, and close. A stop or close request immediately prevents both an
  active runtime and a runtime whose attachment is already underway from
  becoming active afterwards. Once handed to runtime ownership, a runtime is
  released exactly once whether attachment succeeds, is cancelled, closes, or
  fails.
- Accepting an exercise completion immediately removes that runtime's authority
  to publish state, events, actions, or another completion before its
  asynchronous cleanup begins.
- When an operation and its cleanup both fail, the operation failure remains
  the primary reported cause; cleanup failures remain diagnostic information.
- Interaction is enabled only for an active session with an active exercise.
  A completed session is published only once its statistics are ready, at the
  same time as it has no active exercise and disabled interaction.
- Continuing a completed session restores every resource required for active
  training, including keeping the device awake.
- Each exercise runtime generation can record progress at most once; callbacks
  from replaced runtimes cannot affect the active exercise.
- Stopping or leaving training immediately prevents the current exercise from
  advancing to another exercise, clears any pending feedback, and releases
  exercise resources before navigation continues.
- Automatic exit from training continues to navigation after best-effort
  cleanup, including when cleanup reports a failure, and commits that exit at
  most once.
- Starting, continuing, stopping, and leaving a session are safe when invoked
  repeatedly or concurrently: they produce one lifecycle outcome and do not
  leave the learner in an intermediate loading or transition state after an
  operational failure.
- When an exercise, scheduler, progress operation, or resource cleanup fails,
  the trainer presents a recoverable paused state with an actionable error; it
  does not continue to a new exercise automatically.
- If an operation and its recovery both fail, the learner-facing error and the
  reported operation failure remain the original operation failure; recovery
  failure is diagnostic only.
- A new trainer application joins the product family by supplying its own app
  and content while participating in the shared trainer experience.

## Durable Architecture And Constraints

The dependency direction is application to content and shared trainer platform;
content may depend on the shared trainer platform; the shared trainer platform
does not depend on any individual application's branding or content.

## Non-Goals

- Moving app-specific identity or content metadata into the shared platform.
- Making every trainer application use identical content or branding.

## Acceptance Criteria

- NumberGym and VerbGym can use shared learning behavior without sharing their
  branding or subject-specific content.
- A future trainer can be added without requiring the shared platform to depend
  on that trainer's content or identity.
- Completing a session exposes its statistics and permits one subsequent
  Continue action to begin a fresh active session without retaining the prior
  session's statistics.
- Leaving training during feedback, exercise startup, or resource cleanup does
  not start another exercise and does not retain keep-awake or active exercise
  resources.
- A failure while starting or completing an exercise leaves the learner able to
  retry rather than stranded in a loading or transition state.
- A cleanup failure while stopping or leaving training does not prevent the
  learner from leaving the training screen or subsequently starting a session.
- A close concurrent with runtime replacement leaves no active runtime, and
  concurrent runtime replacements leave only the latest attached runtime.
- A stop or close concurrent with attachment leaves no active new runtime, and
  a completion claim immediately prevents late callbacks from restoring the
  completed exercise state.

## Verification

- Automated integration coverage establishes that each application can provide
  its catalog to the shared trainer experience.
- Architecture checks establish the intended dependency direction.
- Automated lifecycle scenarios cover session completion and continuation,
  stopping during feedback or startup, stale exercise callbacks, and failures
  during startup, completion, and cleanup.
- Automated lifecycle scenarios cover idle and repeated stops, recovery from
  failed stop and completion operations, coherent published state snapshots,
  and exactly-once progress for concurrent completion signals.
- Automated runtime-ownership scenarios cover cancellation failure during
  detachment, stop or close concurrent with attachment, late callbacks after a
  completion claim, and concurrent attachments.
